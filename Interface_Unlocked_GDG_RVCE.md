## The challenge

GDG RVCE is building a home for its community: a place to discover events, relive past ones and meet the team. Before development starts, we want to settle on one look and feel, and we want it to come from the students who will use it.

You do not need to design the whole website. Show us your idea on three key pages. Once one design is chosen, it becomes the visual foundation, and the remaining pages are built from it.

## Pages to design

Design these three pages. Use sample content of your own; no real backend is needed.

| Page | What it should show | Where to put your design effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Short introduction to the community, clear calls to action, featured and upcoming events, latest recap, community links | First impression, hero, navigation, overall mood |
| **Events** | An events list with search or filters, and one event detail view with date, time, venue or online details, and a registration link | Cards, filters, hierarchy of information, a memorable event page |
| **Team** | Team profiles with name, role, optional short bio and links | Personality, how people are presented, interaction on cards |

- **Mobile matters.** Show at least Home and Events on a phone-sized screen as well as on desktop.
- **Optional touches.** If they fit your idea, add one or two signature details, such as an event countdown, a gallery view or a light and dark mode. They are not required, and a clear idea beats a long feature list.
- Other pages (About, Resources, FAQ, Join and Contact) are not part of this challenge.

## Design directions you can explore

Pick a direction, or blend a few. Whatever you choose, it must still feel clearly like GDG. Tell us why your style suits the community.

| Style | What it feels like | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Glassmorphism | Frosted, translucent panels over soft colour, light and airy | Low contrast on text over blur |
| Neo-brutalism | Thick outlines, flat bold colour, hard shadows, raw and playful | Staying tidy and readable, not just loud |
| Maximalism | Rich layers, big type, lots of colour and pattern, high energy | Hierarchy getting lost; keep one clear focus |
| Minimal and clean | Generous space, strong grid, restrained colour with sharp accents | Looking generic; add a distinct touch |
| Bento grid | Modular tiles of different sizes, each holding one idea | Overcrowded tiles on mobile |
| Soft 3D or claymorphism | Rounded, tactile shapes with gentle depth and friendly mood | Heavy imagery slowing the page |
| Dark and neon | Dark surfaces with glowing accents, techy and futuristic | Glare and contrast; Google colours must stay recognisable |
| Retro, pixel or terminal | Code-editor, pixel-art or old-computer nostalgia for developers | Becoming a joke; keep it usable |
| Editorial and typographic | Magazine-style layouts led by large, expressive type | Readability on small screens |
| Your own idea | Anything else you can justify and execute well | Clarity of the concept |

## GDG brand guidelines

Your design must follow the official GDG brand guidelines. Take care of them from the first sketch, not at the end. Designs that ignore them will be marked down.

The organizers will share the official guidelines and brand assets with all participants. Until you have them, use a plain placeholder for the logo.

- **Colour.** Build around the four Google colours (blue, red, yellow, green) and use them consistently. Check that text on every colour is readable.
- **Logo.** Use the official GDG logo exactly as supplied. Do not redraw, recolour, stretch or distort it, and give it room to breathe.
- **Name.** Use the chapter's official name as shared by the organizers.
- **Spirit.** Keep the tone open, friendly and developer-focused. Do not present the site as an official Google product.
- **Third-party material.** Do not use other companies' logos or copyrighted artwork. Credit any fonts, icons and images you use.

## Quality bar

- **Responsive.** Layouts that make sense on mobile, tablet and desktop.
- **Accessible.** Readable contrast and text sizes, visible focus states, and meaningful labels and alt text.
- **Light and dark themes.** Both must be readable and consistent.
- **Consistent.** Reusable colours, type, spacing and components, so the design can grow into a full site.
- **Buildable.** Your idea should be realistic to develop. Avoid effects that cannot work on the web or would make pages slow.

## Ground rules

- **Original work.** Design it yourself. Credit any open-source libraries, fonts, icons and images, and make sure you have the right to use them.
- **Sample content only.** Use made-up names, photos and contact details. No real personal data.
- **No tracking features.** Do not include analytics, RSVP or interest counters, attendee lists, comments or live chat.
- **No backend needed.** If you code your design, use static or mock data. Next.js with Tailwind CSS is preferred, because the real site is planned on them, but it is not required.

## How to submit

A complete entry contains:

1. Your design for Home, Events and Team: working frontend code, a public GitHub repository link and a live deployed website link. Figma may be used optionally for designing and prototyping. The final submission must be a public GitHub repository containing the working frontend implementation.
2. Mobile-responsive versions for the respective pages as well. A live deployed website link (Vercel or any other hosting platform).
3. A short design note (one page at most): your chosen style, colour and type choices, and how you followed the GDG brand guidelines.

Submit through the official submission form by **28 November 2026, 11:59 PM IST**. The form link will be shared by the organizers.

## How the winning design is chosen

There is no automated scoring. Organizers review all entries and shortlist the strongest designs. Shortlisted designers then participate in an open group discussion with the GDG RVCE community, where they present their ideas and receive feedback.

The discussion looks at:

- **Concept and originality:** a clear idea that feels fresh and memorable.
- **GDG brand fit:** how well it respects the guidelines and feels like our community.
- **Usability:** clear navigation, strong hierarchy, and a smooth experience on mobile and desktop.
- **Visual craft:** polish, consistency and attention to detail.
- **Accessibility:** contrast, readable type and clear states.
- **Feasibility:** how readily it can be developed into the full website.

Following the discussion, the Core Team makes the final decision, taking the group's feedback into consideration.

## After the challenge: join the build team

Once a design is chosen, development continues from it. We may select the best builders from this challenge to keep working on the GDG RVCE website. Selected builders will work with the core team to turn the chosen design into the full site. Details of roles and how the work is organised will be shared with those selected.

Taking part does not guarantee a place on the build team, and not winning does not rule you out: strong individual contributions can be noticed whatever the final pick.

## FAQ and contact

**Do I need to design every page?**  
No. Home, Events and Team are enough.

**Do I have to write code?**  
Yes. Working frontend code is mandatory. Figma is optional and can be used for prototyping, but a Figma-only submission will not qualify. The final submission must be a public GitHub repository and a live deployed link.

**Can I mix design styles?**  
Yes, as long as the result feels consistent and clearly GDG.

**Can I use the GDG logo?**  
Official brand assets will be shared with participants. Until then, use a placeholder.

**Will my work be used on the real site?**  
The chosen design is the foundation of the official site. Selected builders continue the work with the core team.

**What should my GitHub README include?**  
Include a brief project description, screenshots, technologies used, setup instructions, and a live demo link.
